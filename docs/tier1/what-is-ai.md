# What is AI?

**Artificial Intelligence (AI)** is a broad field of computer science focused on creating systems that can perform tasks typically requiring human intelligence. These include understanding language, recognizing patterns, making decisions, and solving problems.

## The Simple Version

Think of AI as teaching computers to **learn from examples** rather than following rigid instructions. Instead of programming every possible scenario ("if X happens, do Y"), you show the computer thousands of examples and let it figure out the rules itself.

## Three Levels (They Build on Each Other)

| Level | What It Does | Real-World Example |
|-------|-------------|-------------------|
| **AI** (broadest) | Any system that acts "intelligently" | A thermostat that learns your schedule |
| **Machine Learning** | Systems that improve from data | Netflix recommending shows you'll like |
| **Deep Learning** | Uses layered "neural networks" inspired by brains | Siri understanding what you said |

## What AI Is NOT

- **Not magic** — it's math and statistics, just very sophisticated ones
- **Not conscious** — current AI has no feelings, beliefs, or self-awareness
- **Not always right** — AI makes mistakes, sometimes confidently wrong ones (called "hallucinations")
- **Not a single thing** — there are thousands of different AI systems, each built for specific tasks

## Why It Matters Now

AI went mainstream around 2022 when large language models (like the one powering this conversation) became accessible to everyone. Before that, AI was mostly used by big companies for things like fraud detection or search engines. Now it's in your phone, your email, and increasingly in tools you use daily.
